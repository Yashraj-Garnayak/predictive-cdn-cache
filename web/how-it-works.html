How it works

Can a model guess what
people will watch next?

This project replays 8 days of real CDN traffic to find out. Ten short steps, from one request to the final result. Scroll, or press Next.

Step 1 · The problem

Every request: hit or miss

When someone presses play, the request first checks a nearby cache that holds 200 videos. If the video is there, it is a hit and comes back fast; if not, it is a miss and makes the long trip to the origin server.

Step 2 · The data

From raw logs to tables

Meta's real CDN logs go through a cleaning script that keeps the 20,000 most-requested objects in each of 3 edge clusters. Counting those requests per hour shrinks 57 million rows to 2.9 million, stored in PostgreSQL on Azure.

Step 3 · Split by time

Learn first, then get tested

The 193 hours are cut in half. In the first 96 the model learns and the caches fill up; only the last 97 are scored, and at every hour the model may look only at the hours before it.

Step 4 · Building clues

Turning clicks into clues

For each video, the model gets a few numbers about its recent past: requests 1, 2, 3, 6 and 24 hours ago, how fast it is growing, its 6-hour average and the hour of day. The answer it learns is next hour's requests.

Step 5 · Gradient boosting

Many small trees, one guess

The model starts from a simple guess, then adds small decision trees, each one fixing part of the error that is left. Its prediction is the sum of all 200 trees.

Step 6 · The four cache rules

Four rules for a full shelf

The shelf is full and video F arrives. Each rule decides what to throw out. Shown on a 5-slot shelf; the real one has 200.

Step 7 · The hourly loop

The same loop, every hour

For each of the 97 test hours the same six steps repeat: predict, rank, pre-load, replay the real requests, count hits and misses, then move to the next hour.

Step 8 · What we measure

Three ways to keep score

Hit rate scores the cache. Precision@10 and the average error score the model's guesses.

Step 9 · The finding

Recent requests matter most

Plain LRU, which only remembers what was used recently, reaches 51–81% at cache size 200. The model-based rules add at most 0.4 points on top, while LFU, which trusts old popularity, falls far behind.
